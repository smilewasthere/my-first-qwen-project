How VERA scores are calculated
✕

Every score in the VERA database blends a title's real vote history against a dynamic baseline — so a brand-new show and a decade-old giant are judged fairly against each other, not by the same fixed rules.

Try it on a real title
Search any of the 15,200 titles to see exactly how its own score was built.
The formula

VERA Score = (W / (W + M)) × S  +  (M / (W + M)) × C

W
Weight
Real votes behind the title. Completed counts fully; watching, on-hold, dropped, and plan-to-watch count only partially — they're not a finished opinion yet.
S
Score
The title's own average score, after smoothing away suspicious vote-bombing spikes.
M
Baseline weight
Large for new or thin titles, shrinking as a title ages and earns more real votes.
C
Global average
The average score across the whole VERA database — the "neutral" starting point every title leans on until it earns its own weight.

In plain terms: a title with millions of real completions is judged almost entirely on its own votes. A title with only a couple hundred votes leans much more on the baseline, since a handful of votes could easily be noise, hype, or a coordinated attack. As real opinions accumulate, a title earns the right to be judged more and more on its own merit.

Built-in protections
🛡
Anti-bombing smoothing
If the 1s or 10s bucket spikes far above what the surrounding score distribution would predict, that spike is capped before the average is calculated — blunting coordinated vote-bombing without touching genuine, organically extreme opinions.
📡
Airing-title caution
Currently-airing titles get an extra, time-decaying penalty proportional to how much of their vote weight is still speculative (watching, not completed) — this fades the longer a show airs and the more of its audience actually finishes it.
📊
Minimum data floor
Titles under 100 members are marked N/A — there simply isn't enough data for a meaningful score. They're still listed, sorted by member count, but excluded from the numbered ranking.
